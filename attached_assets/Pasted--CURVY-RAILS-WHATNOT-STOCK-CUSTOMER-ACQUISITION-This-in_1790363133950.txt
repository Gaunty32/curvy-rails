# CURVY RAILS — WHATNOT, STOCK & CUSTOMER ACQUISITION

This instruction supplements the main Curvy Rails website brief.

There is an important distinction between our two sales channels that must inform the architecture.

## THE BUSINESS MODEL

Curvy Rails will initially have two very different ways of selling.

### WHATNOT — PRIMARY ACQUISITION CHANNEL

Whatnot live auctions will initially be our main sales activity.

The products sold there will predominantly be:

* one-off garments
* individual pieces
* opportunity purchases
* clearance stock
* items which may never become permanent Curvy Rails products

We DO NOT need Whatnot inventory synchronised with the Curvy Rails website.

### CURVY RAILS WEBSITE — STOCKED RANGES

The website will sell products we deliberately hold in stock across sizes/variants.

These will be repeatable product lines with proper SKU-level inventory.

The website therefore needs its own comprehensive stock-management system.

---

# THE CONNECTION IS THE CUSTOMER — NOT THE STOCK

This is the most important principle.

Do NOT attempt to synchronise Whatnot inventory with Curvy Rails inventory.

Instead, we want to use Whatnot to:

DISCOVER PEOPLE

↓

SELL TO THEM LIVE

↓

INTRODUCE THEM TO CURVY RAILS

↓

GET THEM TO JOIN THE RAILS

↓

BUILD A DIRECT CUSTOMER RELATIONSHIP

↓

INTRODUCE THEM TO STOCKED WEBSITE PRODUCTS

↓

ENCOURAGE COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

↓

GENERATE REPEAT WEBSITE PURCHASES

Whatnot is therefore both a sales channel and an important customer-acquisition channel.

Curvy Rails becomes the customer's longer-term home.

---

# 1. WHATNOT INTEGRATION LAYER

Do not invent Whatnot functionality or assume APIs exist.

Create a clean, isolated Whatnot integration layer that can later use whatever legitimate functionality Whatnot makes available.

We eventually want to investigate whether we can obtain/import appropriate information such as:

* Whatnot username
* order reference
* purchase date
* products purchased
* amount paid
* customer identity information legitimately supplied to the seller
* delivery information where appropriate

We DO NOT currently need:

* Whatnot stock sync
* Whatnot listing sync
* website products pushed to Whatnot
* Whatnot inventory pulled into Curvy Rails

Do not build these.

---

# 2. CUSTOMER IDENTITY

The important challenge is identifying a Whatnot customer when they subsequently join Curvy Rails.

Curvy Rails registration should therefore support:

### Did you find us on Whatnot?

Yes / No

If Yes:

### What's your Whatnot username?

Optional.

Store this privately against the customer profile.

Do not publicly display the Whatnot username unless the member explicitly chooses to do so.

Architecture should allow an administrator eventually to match:

WHATNOT BUYER

to

CURVY RAILS MEMBER

where there is sufficient legitimate evidence to do so.

Never guess customer matches.

---

# 3. JOIN THE RAILS

The primary Whatnot-to-website CTA should NOT necessarily be:

"Shop our website."

It should be:

# JOIN THE RAILS

We are inviting someone into the Curvy Rails community.

Once registered they gain:

* My Rail
* Rail Stars
* My Wallet
* Ask the Rails
* community participation
* purchase history
* stocked Curvy Rails products
* future community benefits

This gives customers a reason to register even if they don't immediately want to buy something else.

---

For example:

Persist useful first-party acquisition attribution against:

# 6. LIVE EVENT MANAGEMENT

Create a simple Live Event model/admin feature.

Admin should be able to set:

* Live / Not Live
* title
* description
* external Whatnot URL
* scheduled date/time
* optional image
* campaign code

When Live = true:

The website can display a subtle:

🔴 CURVY RAILS IS LIVE

notification/banner.

Clicking it should take visitors to /live.

Do not automatically embed Whatnot unless we later confirm this is technically and contractually appropriate.

Examples:

# 8. WEBSITE STOCK MANAGEMENT

The WEBSITE requires proper stock control.

This is independent from Whatnot one-off auction inventory.

Every stocked Curvy Rails website product should have variants/SKUs.

Example:

Product:

Floral Midi Dress

Variants:

CR-1001-GRN-16
CR-1001-GRN-18
CR-1001-GRN-20
CR-1001-GRN-22
CR-1001-GRN-24
etc.

For each SKU track:

* product
* size
* colour
* SKU
* barcode if applicable
* supplier
* cost price
* retail price
* physical stock
* reserved stock
* available stock
* incoming stock
* reorder level
* location if applicable

Do not hard-code size ranges.

---

# 9. STOCK LEDGER

Do not simply overwrite stock quantity.

Create an auditable Stock Movement ledger.

Movement types should support:

PURCHASE_RECEIPT
WEBSITE_SALE
CUSTOMER_RETURN
ORDER_CANCELLED
DAMAGED
LOST
MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT
STOCKTAKE_CORRECTION
RESERVATION
RESERVATION_RELEASE

Each movement should record:

* SKU
* quantity
* movement type
* date/time
* reference/order
* user/system responsible
* notes

We should always be able to answer:

**Why does the system say we have six of these?**

---

# 10. STOCK ADMIN

# STOCK

TOTAL STOCK UNITS

OUT OF STOCK
INCOMING
STOCK VALUE

Search/filter by:

* SKU
* product
* size
* colour
* category
* supplier
* stock status

Individual product/SKU history should show stock movements.

---

# 11. WEBSITE RESERVATIONS

Website checkout must protect against overselling.

Concept:

AVAILABLE STOCK

↓

Customer begins checkout

↓

TEMPORARY RESERVATION

↓

Payment succeeds

↓

WEBSITE SALE

or:

Payment fails/expires

↓

RESERVATION RELEASED

Reservation timeout should be configurable.

Use proper database transaction handling to avoid simultaneous orders creating negative inventory.

---

# 12. STOCKTAKE

Build architecture for simple stocktaking.

Admin should be able to:

* start stocktake
* enter/count SKU
* compare expected vs actual
* see discrepancy
* approve adjustment

Any adjustment must create a Stock Movement.

Do not silently overwrite inventory.

---

# 13. WHATNOT CUSTOMERS AND MY RAIL

Longer term, if we can reliably establish that a registered member made a purchase through Whatnot, allow the architecture to record this.

Potential My Rail display:

### Bought on the Live ✓

This does NOT mean the Whatnot product has to become website stock.

We simply want the customer relationship/history to recognise that Curvy Rails originally met them through Whatnot.

Do not build automatic verification until the available Whatnot data has been established.

# 14. WEBSITE VERIFIED OWNERSHIP

Can contribute:

Do not assume that a Whatnot one-off purchase maps to a website product.

---

# 15. HIGHLEVEL CRM

HighLevel will ultimately become the communication/CRM layer.

The website should eventually be able to classify customers/contacts such as:

* Whatnot sourced
* Curvy Rails member
* website customer
* repeat website customer
* community contributor
* verified owner
* Rail Stars participant

HighLevel should NOT become the master database for orders or inventory.

Curvy Rails remains the source of truth for commerce/community information.

HighLevel receives the customer/marketing information necessary for communication and automation.

Do not connect HighLevel yet.

---

# 16. CUSTOMER CONSENT

Acquiring a customer through Whatnot does NOT automatically mean they have consented to Curvy Rails marketing.

Keep separate records for:

* account/service communications
* marketing email consent
* SMS consent where relevant
* other marketing channels

Do not automatically subscribe imported Whatnot customers to marketing.

The preferred journey is to encourage them to actively:

# JOIN THE RAILS

and make their own communication choices.

# THE IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

**Whatnot inventory and website inventory are separate.**

**The customer relationship is what connects the two.**

Whatnot helps us find customers.

Curvy Rails gives those customers somewhere to belong.

The website gives them stocked products to purchase outside the live auctions.

The community gives them a reason to keep coming back.
