# CURVY RAILS — WEBSITE & COMMUNITY BUILD BRIEF

We are adding a new customer-facing website called **Curvy Rails** to this existing project.

Before writing or changing any code:

1. Inspect the existing project structure, database, authentication, integrations and existing applications.
2. Identify the safest way to add Curvy Rails without breaking, replacing or materially altering existing functionality.
3. Reuse existing infrastructure where sensible, but keep Curvy Rails logically separated.
4. Do not delete existing functionality, tables, data, integrations or routes.
5. Do not connect live payment, email, HighLevel or other external services without approval.
6. Build using test/demo data initially.
7. Report your proposed architecture and any risks before making significant structural changes.

---

# 1. THE IDEA

Curvy Rails is an ecommerce website selling plus-size clothing to men and women.

The current brand covers approximately UK sizes **16–32**, although the platform must not hard-code that range because it may expand.

This must NOT feel like a generic commercial plus-size ecommerce website.

Curvy Rails is being built around an existing community originating largely from **Whatnot**.

The website should therefore feel:

* welcoming
* independent
* British
* community-led
* informal
* warm
* non-judgemental
* trustworthy
* fun
* human

It should NOT feel:

* corporate
* clinical
* like a generic Shopify template
* obsessed with weight/body size
* overly polished
* like a discount clothing warehouse

The underlying idea is:

## REAL CLOTHES. REAL PEOPLE. REAL HELP.

Curvy Rails isn't simply somewhere to buy clothes.

It is somewhere people help one another buy clothes.

# 2. BRAND

Brand name:

# Curvy Rails

Existing logo will be supplied and should be incorporated into the design.

Do not redesign the logo at this stage.

The visual identity should complement the playful, bold nature of the existing Curvy Rails logo while allowing the website itself to remain modern and easy to navigate.

Use plenty of whitespace, warm neutral backgrounds and restrained accent colours.

Avoid stereotypical "plus-size" branding.

Do not make everything pink.

Photography should feature genuine-looking men and women with different body shapes and ages.

People should look like customers rather than fashion models.

# 6. VERIFIED OWNERSHIP

This is extremely important.

The system must know whether a registered customer has genuinely purchased a particular product.

A user becomes a:

## VERIFIED OWNER

when their account can be matched against an order containing that product.

Only Verified Owners may:

* leave product experience/feedback
* upload photographs against that product
* earn feedback-related Rail Stars
* answer owner-specific questions where required

Do not rely solely on the customer claiming they own something.

Design the data model so historical purchases from sources such as Whatnot could eventually be imported and associated with customer accounts.

---

# 7. FROM THE RAILS

Do NOT call this section "Reviews".

Call it:

# FROM THE RAILS

This is product experience rather than conventional star-rating reviewing.

Only verified purchasers may contribute.

Example:

**Sophie — Verified Owner ✓**

Bought: Size 22
Usually wears: Size 22
Fit: Just right

> Absolutely love this dress. So comfortable and it flows really nicely. I'm 5'7" and it's the perfect length for me.

Customers should optionally be able to provide structured information such as:

* size purchased
* size normally worn
* fit
* length
* height
* useful fit observations

These should be optional wherever appropriate.

Do NOT require customers to provide body measurements.

---

# 8. CUSTOMER PHOTOGRAPHS

Verified owners can optionally upload photographs of themselves wearing a purchased product.

Create a product section called:

# WORN ON THE RAILS

These photographs should appear alongside the customer's product experience.

Photos must have:

* upload
* moderation status
* reporting
* removal capability
* privacy controls
* image optimisation
* appropriate file/type limits

Customers must explicitly agree that an uploaded image may be displayed publicly on Curvy Rails.

Do NOT implement public comments underneath customer photographs.

Do NOT create appearance ratings.

Do NOT create emoji reactions to someone's body/image.

A visitor may indicate that the accompanying **information was helpful**, but should not publicly rate the person in the photograph.

The fundamental rule is:

## We talk about the clothes. We don't judge the person wearing them.

---

# 9. ASK THE RAILS

Every product should have:

# ASK THE RAILS

Anyone may READ questions and answers.

Only registered users may ASK a question.

Questions can be asked about ANY product; purchase is not required.

Examples:

"Does this come up long?"

"How stretchy is the waistband?"

"Would you size up in this?"

"Is the material quite heavy?"

Design the system so verified owners of that particular product can be notified when someone asks a question.

Example notification:

### Someone needs your help.

Sarah is thinking about buying something you already own.

"Does this blouse cling around the stomach or is it fairly loose?"

**Answer Sarah →**

Where possible, answers from verified owners should be clearly marked:

**Verified Owner ✓**

---

# 10. COMMUNITY SAFETY

Curvy Rails must be designed as a:

# JUDGEMENT-FREE RAIL

Build moderation/reporting functionality into community content from the start.

Community content should be reportable.

Administrators must be able to:

* hide content
* approve content
* reject content
* remove content
* suspend community privileges
* review reported content
* remove photographs
* reverse rewards where necessary

The system should discourage:

* body shaming
* personal insults
* sexual comments
* comments about someone's appearance
* harassment
* discriminatory content
* spam

Negative PRODUCT feedback must NOT automatically be treated as undesirable.

For example:

"This comes up very small. I'd go up a size."

is useful community information.

The platform should reward usefulness, not positivity.

---

# 11. "THAT HELPED"

Useful feedback and answers should have:

### 👍 That helped

Registered members can indicate that an answer or product experience helped them.

Do not create dislike/downvote functionality.

This metric can eventually help surface particularly useful contributions.

---

# 12. RAIL STARS

Create a community reward system called:

# RAIL STARS ⭐

This is NOT a traditional spend-based loyalty programme.

Customers DO NOT earn stars simply because they spend more money.

The principle is:

# YOU DON'T EARN MORE BECAUSE YOU SPEND MORE.

# YOU EARN MORE BECAUSE YOU HELP MORE.

Rail Stars can be earned through useful community participation.

Make reward values configurable by administrators rather than hard-coded.

Initial example rules could be:

* Verified product experience submitted: +10 stars
* Genuine customer photograph added: +10 stars
* Answer a product question: +5 stars
* Answer marked "That helped": bonus +5 stars

These are initial examples only.

Admin must be able to change reward values.

---

# 13. STAR WALLET

Inside My Rail create:

# MY WALLET

Example:

⭐ **145 Rail Stars**

**You've helped 12 people on the Rails.**

Display:

* current stars
* stars earned
* stars redeemed
* pending stars
* reward history
* available account credit

Initially model:

**100 Rail Stars = £5 Curvy Rails credit**

BUT make this administrator configurable.

Do not hard-code the conversion.

Rail Stars themselves should not have a cash withdrawal value.

---

# 14. ANTI-ABUSE

Rail Stars must not encourage spam.

Therefore:

* reward once per eligible action
* verify purchase before purchase-feedback rewards
* detect duplicate submissions
* moderation can remove stars
* deleted/rejected contributions can reverse rewards
* configurable daily/activity limits
* maintain a complete reward ledger

Never simply store a single editable "stars balance".

Use a proper transaction/ledger model so every award and redemption has an audit trail.

↓

↓

Earns Rail Stars

↓

Uploads optional photograph

↓

Earns additional stars

↓

Another customer asks a question

↓

Verified owners notified

↓

Owner answers

↓

Earns Rail Stars

↓

Questioner marks answer "That helped"

↓

Owner potentially earns bonus stars

This loop is central to Curvy Rails.

Build the database and events with this future automation in mind.
