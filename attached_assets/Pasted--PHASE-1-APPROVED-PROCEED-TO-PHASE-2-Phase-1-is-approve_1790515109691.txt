# PHASE 1 APPROVED — PROCEED TO PHASE 2

Phase 1 is approved.

The separation between:

* sellable variants
* physical components
* BOMs
* component stock movements
* legacy finished stock

is correct.

Do not alter that principle.

Proceed with **Phase 2: Component Availability, Checkout Holds, Confirmed Allocations and Shortfalls**.

Do NOT proceed into purchasing, production or despatch yet.

---

# 1. PHASE 2 OBJECTIVE

At the end of Phase 2 the system must be able to answer reliably:

### Can we make this product right now?

### If a customer starts checkout, can we temporarily protect the required components?

### Once payment is confirmed, can we allocate those components to that order?

### If something is unavailable, exactly what are we short of?

### If several sellable products use the same blank, are they all competing for the same real stock?

These are the Phase 2 success criteria.

---

# 2. COMPONENT AVAILABILITY

For each physical component calculate separately:

**PHYSICAL**

**TEMPORARILY HELD**

**CONFIRMED ALLOCATED**

**AVAILABLE**

Where:

AVAILABLE = PHYSICAL − ACTIVE HOLDS − CONFIRMED ALLOCATIONS

Do not change physical stock merely because something has been held or allocated.

Physical stock should ultimately reduce at production consumption, not at checkout.

---

# 3. BOM AVAILABILITY

For a sellable variant, explode its CURRENT ACTIVE BOM.

For every physical component calculate how many finished units that component can support.

Example:

Black Hoodie Size 22:

8 available

Good Vibes Transfer:

23 available

Each finished hoodie requires:

1 hoodie
1 transfer

Immediate producible quantity:

# 8

If a component quantity in the BOM were 2 per finished unit, use the appropriate whole-number calculation.

Non-stock production operations such as:

HEAT PRESS

EMBROIDERY OPERATION

APPLICATION POSITION

must NOT constrain physical availability.

---

# 4. SHARED COMPONENT COMPETITION

This is critical.

Suppose:

Black Hoodie Size 22 = 10 available.

It is shared by:

Good Vibes Hoodie
Curvy Rails Hoodie
Friday Night Hoodie

Each individual product may technically be capable of producing 10 units before any reservation exists.

However, there are only TEN physical blanks.

If a customer reserves:

6 × Good Vibes Hoodie

the other products must immediately see only:

4 blanks

available to support their BOMs.

Do not create virtual stock pools per design.

---

# 5. WEBSITE DISPLAY

Do not necessarily expose exact component-derived quantities to the customer.

Create an availability service capable of returning appropriate customer-facing states such as:

**IN STOCK**

**LOW STOCK**

**AVAILABLE TO ORDER**

**OUT OF STOCK**

Keep the underlying numeric availability internally.

Thresholds/policies should be configurable rather than scattered through UI code.

---

# 6. IMPORTANT — AVAILABLE TO ORDER

Do NOT implement unlimited back-ordering.

Create an explicit policy.

A sellable variant/component combination may eventually permit:

# AVAILABLE TO ORDER

where we are confident components can be replenished within the promised lead time.

For Phase 2, build the architecture and policy fields required for this.

However, unless explicitly configured otherwise:

**insufficient component availability must prevent immediate-stock checkout.**

Do not silently assume that because we have a supplier, we can obtain something.

---

# 7. CHECKOUT HOLD

When a customer reaches the appropriate checkout stage, create a:

# TEMPORARY COMPONENT HOLD

The hold must contain:

* session/customer reference
* cart/order reference
* sellable variant
* BOM version
* component
* quantity
* created timestamp
* expiry timestamp
* status

Suggested statuses:

ACTIVE

CONVERTED

RELEASED

EXPIRED

Do not reserve an imaginary finished garment.

Reserve the actual physical BOM components.

---

# 8. HOLD EXPIRY

Holds must automatically cease affecting availability after expiry.

Make the hold duration configurable.

Do not rely exclusively on a scheduled cleanup job to restore availability.

Availability calculations must recognise an expired hold as expired even if housekeeping has not physically cleaned the row yet.

This prevents stale holds from blocking stock.

---

# 9. ATOMIC RESERVATION

This is one of the most important technical requirements.

Two customers must NOT both successfully reserve the final physical component.

Example:

1 × Black Hoodie Size 24 remains.

Customer A attempts checkout.

Customer B attempts checkout simultaneously.

Only ONE may successfully obtain the hold.

Use proper database transaction/locking/concurrency controls.

Do not solve this using only:

"read quantity → check → write hold"

without appropriate locking.

---

# 10. CONFIRMED PAYMENT

When payment is genuinely confirmed:

TEMPORARY HOLD

becomes:

# CONFIRMED COMPONENT ALLOCATION

The confirmed allocation must retain:

* order
* order item
* sellable variant
* BOM version/snapshot
* component
* quantity
* allocation timestamp
* status

Do not reduce physical component stock at this point.

Instead:

Physical = unchanged

Allocated = increased

Available = reduced

Later production consumption will deal with the physical deduction.

---

# 11. BOM SNAPSHOT

The confirmed order must retain the BOM/version applicable when the customer purchased.

Example:

Monday:

Good Vibes Hoodie uses Transfer A.

Customer orders.

Wednesday:

Admin changes future product to Transfer B.

Monday's order must STILL require:

Transfer A.

Do not recalculate an existing paid order using the newest BOM.

---

# 12. PAYMENT IDEMPOTENCY

Payment confirmation must be retry-safe.

The same payment/provider callback may be received more than once.

It must NOT:

* create another order
* create duplicate allocations
* double reserve components
* create duplicate shortfalls.

Use durable idempotency/reference constraints.

Do not rely only on application-level "if already processed" logic.

---

# 13. ABANDONED / FAILED CHECKOUT

If checkout is abandoned or payment fails:

ACTIVE HOLD

↓

RELEASED / EXPIRED

↓

Components become available again.

This should immediately affect all sellable variants using those components.

---

# 14. CUSTOMER CANCELLATION

If a confirmed order is cancelled before its components have been consumed:

CONFIRMED ALLOCATION

↓

RELEASED

↓

Components become available again.

Do not create a physical stock increase because physical stock was never reduced.

This distinction is important.

---

# 15. SHORTFALL ENGINE

For confirmed orders where the configured policy permits order-to-replenish:

calculate component shortfalls.

Example:

Order requires:

4 × Black Hoodie Size 22
4 × Good Vibes Transfer

Available at allocation:

Black Hoodie = 2
Transfer = 20

Allocate:

2 × Black Hoodie
4 × Transfer

Shortfall:

# 2 × BLACK HOODIE SIZE 22

The system should know:

Order requirement = 4

Allocated = 2

Shortfall = 2

Do not simply mark the entire order "out of stock".

---

# 16. DO NOT PURCHASE YET

Phase 2 should identify shortfalls.

It should NOT yet:

* create supplier POs
* send supplier orders
* calculate target-stock replenishment
* receive goods
* move production jobs.

Those belong to subsequent phases.

However, design the shortfall output so Phase 3 purchasing can consume it cleanly.

---

# 17. ORDER READINESS

Introduce a reliable internal readiness calculation.

An order/order line should be able to report:

# FULLY ALLOCATED

Everything required is allocated.

or:

# PARTIALLY ALLOCATED

Some required components are allocated but others are short.

or:

# WAITING FOR COMPONENTS

Required physical components are unavailable.

Do not confuse this with production status yet.

This is component readiness.

# 20. DO NOT MIX HOLDS WITH THE STOCK LEDGER

A temporary checkout hold is NOT a physical stock movement.

A confirmed allocation is NOT a physical stock movement.

Therefore do not create misleading physical ledger entries such as:

CHECKOUT −1

The physical ledger remains about actual physical changes.

Keep:

PHYSICAL MOVEMENTS

and:

RESERVATIONS / ALLOCATIONS

separate but reconcilable.

---

# 21. LEGACY STOCK

Do not switch legacy variant quantities over automatically.

Only products/components explicitly configured for the new BOM/component model should use Phase 2 component availability.

Legacy products must continue to behave as they currently do until intentionally migrated.

We need a controlled coexistence period.

# 23. REQUIRED TESTS

Before Phase 2 is complete demonstrate tests for:

### Basic availability

1. Blank 10 / transfer 20 = producible 10.
2. Blank 20 / transfer 7 = producible 7.
3. Component BOM quantity >1 calculates correctly.
4. Non-stock operation does not constrain availability.

### Shared components

5. Two products share the same blank.
6. Reservation against Product A immediately reduces availability supporting Product B.

### Holds

7. Hold reduces available but not physical stock.
8. Expired hold stops affecting availability.
9. Released hold restores availability.
10. Failed checkout releases hold.

### Concurrency

11. Two simultaneous customers compete for the final component.
12. Only one reservation succeeds.

### Payment

13. Successful payment converts hold to confirmed allocation.
14. Physical stock remains unchanged.
15. Duplicate payment callback cannot duplicate allocation.

### Cancellation

16. Cancellation releases confirmed allocation.
17. Physical stock does not incorrectly increase.

### BOM history

18. Existing paid order retains original BOM after product BOM changes.

### Shortfalls

19. Partial component availability creates correct allocation and shortfall.
20. Multiple missing components produce independent shortfalls.

### Legacy

21. Legacy non-BOM product continues existing behaviour.

Do not only test the UI.

Test concurrency and inventory invariants at database/service level.
